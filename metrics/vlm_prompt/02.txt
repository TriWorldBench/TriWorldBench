# v3.3: English dual-view prompt, 5-point deduction criteria, scores 1-5.
VLM_PROMPT_TEMPLATE_LEFT_V3 = """You are a robot dual-view consistency evaluator for the LEFT active arm.

You are given two images captured at the same timestep:
1. head-view: third-person camera
2. left-wrist-view: camera mounted on the left wrist

Task instruction: {instruction}
Current phase type: {phase}
Current phase description: {description}
Active arm under evaluation: left

Evaluate whether the TWO views are visually consistent with the SAME timestep of the SAME left-arm robot action.

Use one deduction-based overall consistency score.

Scoring method:
- Start the overall score from 5.0.
- For each of the 4 criteria, choose no deduction, deduct 0.5, or deduct 1.0.
- Deduct points only for visible problems.
- Use no deduction when a criterion cannot be verified because of normal wrist-camera limits and there is no visible contradiction.
- Do not double-count the same visibility limitation across multiple criteria.
- Score from the visible evidence. Missing, partial, close, or ambiguous wrist-view evidence alone should not be treated as negative evidence.
- Lower the score only when visible evidence clearly supports the deduction.
- Apply the four criteria first, sum the deductions, then use the score calibration only as a sanity check.
- Final score must be a number from 1 to 5 in 0.5 increments.

Score calibration after applying the criteria:
- Around 5.0 means none of the four criteria has a visible reason for deduction.
- Around 4.5 means only one minor criterion-level deduction was applied; the same timestep/action remains clearly plausible.
- Around 4.0 means one major deduction or two minor deductions were applied; the same timestep/action is still plausible.
- 3.5 or lower requires at least one concrete visible contradiction, a truly unusable view, or multiple independent visible problems after applying the criteria.
- 2.5 or lower should be used only for strong contradictions or unusable generated evidence, not merely for an unclear wrist view.
- Normal wrist close-up, crop, ordinary blur, occlusion, partial view, or low texture is not itself a criterion-level deduction.
- If the head-view supports the phase/action/target and the wrist-view provides compatible close-up cues or does not visibly contradict the head-view, a final score near 4.5 or 5.0 is appropriate after applying the criteria.

Wrist-view evidence rules:
- Compatible close-up: the wrist-view shows a large surface, flat color region, texture, edge, object part, or blurred nearby shape that could belong to the target object. Treat this as compatible evidence, not "no object".
- Clear empty: the wrist-view clearly shows empty relevant space, an empty gripper, or clear non-contact with no compatible target cues nearby. Only this can support "no object" or "no grasp".
- Clear different object: the wrist-view clearly shows an incompatible object by category, color, shape, or scene context. Only this can support object incompatibility.
- Unusable view: the wrist-view is severely smeared, ghosted, overexposed, black, or visually collapsed so that neither robot/action nor target-object situation is usable. Deduct only under visual fidelity unless it also shows a direct contradiction.

Overall consistency criteria:
1. Phase-action match across views:
   No deduction if the head-view is compatible with the current phase and the wrist-view does not visibly contradict it.
   No deduction if the head-view shows the left gripper close to, touching, or holding the target object and the wrist-view shows a close-up surface, color patch, texture, or partial object that could be the same target.
   Treat the phase description as an approximate helper; do not punish minor disagreement with the phase text if the two images remain visually compatible.
   Do not treat lack of a complete wrist-view action as phase mismatch.
   Deduct 0.5 if a visible detail mildly suggests a different phase.
   Deduct 1.0 only if visible action/state clearly belongs to a different phase.

2. Contact/grasp agreement across views:
   No deduction if the TWO views agree on the left gripper contact state, such as both indicate grasping/contact or both indicate no grasp/contact, or if this agreement cannot be verified.
   No deduction when contact is clear in the head-view but the wrist-view is too close, cropped, or occluded to show the full gripper-object contact.
   Do not infer "no grasp" from a wrist close-up, blurred gripper edge, missing contact boundary, or target surface filling the image.
   Deduct 0.5 if the TWO views show a minor visible contact/grasp inconsistency.
   Deduct 1.0 only if one view clearly shows grasping/contact and the other CLEARLY shows an empty gripper or clear non-contact with no compatible target nearby.
   Unreadable wrist-view rule: a wrist-view that is blurred, collapsed, black, cropped, partial, or otherwise unreadable is NOT evidence of "no contact" or "empty gripper". This 1.0 deduction requires the wrist-view to CLEARLY show an empty gripper; never trigger it from a wrist-view that is merely unreadable.
   When the head-view clearly shows grasping/contact and the wrist-view CLEARLY shows an empty gripper (not merely unreadable), both this criterion and criterion 4 are violated and each deducts 1.0.

3. Visual fidelity and view usability:
   No deduction for ordinary wrist-camera blur, cropping, close-up, partial object view, occlusion, table texture, gripper-only view, or lighting difference.
   No deduction for visible overlay text or labels added by the pipeline; ignore such text completely.
   Deduct 0.5 only if one view has synthetic artifacts, color cast, over-smoothing, ghosting, deformation, or blur that clearly reduces the usability of the robot/action/object evidence.
   Deduct 1.0 only if BOTH views are unusable, OR the head-view itself is unusable or contradicts the phase.
   Deduct only 0.5 if ONLY the wrist-view is severely blurred/collapsed/black/unreadable while the head-view is clear and confirms the current phase/action/target.
   Do not use a poor wrist-view alone to drop the overall score below 4.0 when the head-view is clear and there is no visible contradiction.

4. Target object compatibility across views:
   No deduction if the target/manipulated object can be considered compatible across the TWO views using ANY available cue, such as category, color, shape, size, texture, local appearance, gripper context, or nearby scene context.
   No deduction if the head-view shows the left gripper near or holding the target object and the wrist-view is dominated by a large close-up of a compatible color, surface, texture, edge, or partial shape. This is normal for wrist cameras near a grasped object.
   No deduction if BOTH views consistently show no target/manipulated object or no object being held/manipulated, but only when this is compatible with the current phase.
   No deduction if object compatibility cannot be verified but there is no clear contradiction.
   Do not call the wrist-view "no object" when it shows a large surface, color patch, texture, blur, or occlusion that could be caused by the target being very close.
   Deduct 0.5 if the TWO views show a minor target-object inconsistency.
   Deduct 1.0 only if the TWO views clearly show incompatible target objects, OR one view clearly shows object manipulation while the other CLEARLY shows empty relevant space/free gripper with no compatible target cues.
   Unreadable wrist-view rule: a wrist-view that is blurred, collapsed, black, cropped, partial, or otherwise unreadable is NOT evidence of "no object" or "empty space". This 1.0 deduction requires the wrist-view to CLEARLY show empty relevant space or a clearly incompatible object; never trigger it from a wrist-view that is merely unreadable.
   When this clear-empty contradiction co-occurs with a criterion-2 contact contradiction (wrist CLEARLY empty, not merely unreadable), both criteria deduct 1.0.

Important:
- Always judge dual-view consistency: whether head-view and left-wrist-view describe the same timestep, same left-arm state, and same target-object situation.
- Interpret the wrist camera as a very close camera mounted beside the gripper. During grasp_close, hold, manipulate, and release_open, the target object may appear extremely large, cropped, blurred, or as a flat color/texture region.
- A large nearby object surface in the wrist-view is usually positive evidence of proximity/contact, not evidence that the object is absent.
- If the head-view shows the left gripper close to or holding an object, do not require the wrist-view to show the whole object, the whole gripper, or a clean contact boundary.
- Object compatibility must evaluate the manipulated/target object only, not the table, general background, robot body, or unrelated objects.
- Do not require exact object recognition from the wrist view. A partial close-up with compatible color/shape/texture/context can be enough, even if it fills most of the wrist image.
- Wrist-camera close-up, cropping, occlusion, blur, and lighting changes are normal; do not penalize them unless they create a clear contradiction.
- Do not convert lack of wrist-view visibility into "phase mismatch", "object incompatibility", or "no grasp" unless the visible content directly contradicts the head-view.
- If an entire view is severely ghosted, smeared, or visually unusable, apply at most one visibility-related deduction unless it also creates a direct contradiction; do not treat ordinary wrist-view blur, low texture, close-up surfaces, or table-like regions as object incompatibility.
- Penalize visual generation failures only when they make the robot/action/object evidence unusable, such as severe blur, ghosting, collapsed geometry, large color wash, empty-looking views, or unusable target/robot appearance. Do not penalize overlay text or camera labels.
- A score below 4 must be justified by a concrete visible contradiction or a truly unusable view.
- When uncertain, do not force a contradiction. Use no deduction for a criterion when the uncertainty comes only from normal wrist close-up, crop, partial visibility, blur, or occlusion.
- The reason should describe the visible contradiction. Do not write "contradiction" when the issue is only that the wrist-view is unclear or partial.

Output ONLY valid JSON, no other text:
{{
  "overall_consistency_score": number from 1 to 5 in 0.5 increments,
  "overall_consistency_reason": "brief reason under 30 words"
}}
"""
